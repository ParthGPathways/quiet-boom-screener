# Quiet Boom Screener

Which US industries are growing fast **without** an AI narrative attached — and is
the market underpricing that growth?

A full pipeline from public sources to a ranked, interactive answer: scrape the
index constituents, pull a decade of prices and quarterly filings, load it into
SQLite, score every industry, and render a dashboard.

**Universe:** S&P 500 + S&P 400 (903 companies)
**Data:** 2.5m daily prices (yfinance), 353k quarterly fundamentals (SEC EDGAR)
**Stack:** Python · pandas · SQLite · Jinja2

---

## The result

Industries are ranked on three independent screens:

| screen | question | measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Boom** | is it growing faster than it normally does? | median company revenue growth, recent vs long-run baseline, confirmed by margin expansion and capex |
| **Quiet** | has an AI story been attached to it? | two independent measures: correlation with an AI basket after market movement is removed (what investors believe), and AI mentions per 1,000 words of the companies' own 10-K business sections (what they say about themselves) |
| **Value** | is the growth already priced in? | EV/EBITDA against peers, plus a simplified DCF |

**Utilities** come out as the strongest candidate: revenue growth accelerating,
capex up 4–6 points of revenue (the data-centre power build-out), EV/EBITDA of
11–12x, and share prices that move *against* the AI trade.

## Running it

```bash
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt

python src/rebuild.py            # regenerate the site from the database   (~4s)
python src/rebuild.py --data     # + reload the database from raw files    (~7s)
python src/rebuild.py --fetch    # + re-download everything from source    (~15min)
```

Then open `site/index.html`.

`data/` is not in version control — it is regenerated by the scripts.

## Notes on the data

Financial filings are messier than they look, and most of the code exists because
of specific failures found along the way:

- **The SEC reports three shapes of number.** Income statement items give discrete
  quarters; cash flow items are cumulative year-to-date and must be differenced;
  balance sheet items are snapshots with no period at all. Each needs its own rule.
- **Most companies never tag Q4.** Their annual report states the full year and
  leaves the fourth quarter implicit — it has to be derived by subtraction, or 45%
  of December quarters go missing.
- **"Take the newest filing" is right for a value and wrong for a difference.**
  Subtracting an un-restated nine-month figure from a restated annual one produced
  a quarter of $11m for a company that actually earned $8bn. ~1,200 such residuals
  are detected and dropped rather than stored.
- **Acquisitions look exactly like booms** in a single year of revenue. Scoring on
  acceleration against an industry's own baseline, and requiring the growth to be
  broad-based, discounts them.
- **Joins can silently multiply rows.** Dual-class tickers (GOOG/GOOGL) share one
  CIK, so every join to company data goes through a one-row-per-filer view.

## Limitations

- The keyword screen reads Item 1 (Business) only, and 8% of filings structure that
  section in a way the parser cannot locate; those companies are recorded as missing
  rather than measured over a different section.
- The AI basket's members are themselves in the universe, so their industries are
  slightly self-correlated. Measured at -0.004 to -0.064, which changes no ordering.
- EV/EBITDA is not meaningful for banks and insurers; those rest on the DCF alone.
- The DCF is deliberately simple and favours low-beta defensives, which receive a
  lower discount rate.
- Sub-industries with fewer than 5 companies are excluded as too thin to generalise.
